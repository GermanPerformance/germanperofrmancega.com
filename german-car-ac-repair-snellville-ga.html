What You Get

GERMAN CAR AC
SERVICES WE OFFER

We handle every level of AC repair — from a simple recharge to full system replacement.

01
AC System Diagnostic
We perform a full AC system diagnostic including pressure testing, temperature output measurement, and a scan for HVAC fault codes — identifying the root cause before recommending any repair.
02
Refrigerant Recharge
We evacuate and recharge your AC system with the correct refrigerant spec for your vehicle — R134a or the newer R1234yf used in most post-2017 European vehicles. We never just top off a leaking system.
03
Leak Detection & Repair
AC refrigerant leaks are found using UV dye and electronic leak detectors. We locate leaks at the compressor, condenser, evaporator, hoses, and O-rings — then repair them properly so the charge holds.
04
Compressor Replacement
A seized or failing AC compressor is the most common major AC repair. We replace compressors with OEM or OEM-equivalent units and flush the system to remove metal debris before recharging.
05
Condenser & Evaporator Service
A damaged condenser (from road debris) or a leaking evaporator core requires careful diagnosis. We handle both — including the labor-intensive evaporator replacement that requires dashboard removal on many German vehicles.
06
HVAC Control & Blend Door Repair
Dual-zone climate control issues, blend door actuator failures, and blower motor problems are common on aging German vehicles. We diagnose and repair HVAC control systems with the correct diagnostic software for each brand.

Common Questions

GERMAN CAR AC REPAIR FAQ

Why is my German car's AC not blowing cold?

The most common causes are low refrigerant from a leak, a failed AC compressor, a clogged or damaged condenser, or a faulty expansion valve. Less commonly, it can be an electrical issue with the HVAC control module or a blend door actuator failure. We diagnose the root cause before recommending any repair — we don't just recharge and hope for the best.

Can you just recharge my AC without fixing the leak?

We won't. Recharging a leaking system without fixing the leak is a waste of money — the refrigerant will escape again, usually within weeks. We locate and repair the leak first, then recharge. This is the only approach that actually solves the problem.

What refrigerant does my German car use?

Most German vehicles built before 2017 use R134a refrigerant. Most post-2017 European vehicles use the newer R1234yf refrigerant, which requires different equipment and is more expensive per pound. We handle both. We confirm the correct spec for your vehicle before any AC work begins.

How much does AC repair cost on a German car?

It depends entirely on what's failed. A recharge after a minor O-ring leak is relatively inexpensive. A compressor replacement or evaporator core job is significantly more — especially on vehicles that require dashboard removal. We give you a full diagnosis and written estimate before any work starts, so there are no surprises.

My AC works sometimes but not others — what does that mean?

Intermittent AC is usually caused by a failing compressor clutch, a pressure sensor issue, or an electrical fault in the HVAC control system. On German vehicles with dual-zone climate control, it can also be a blend door actuator that's sticking. We'll diagnose it properly with the right scan tools rather than guessing.

Do you work on AC for all German brands?

Yes. We service AC systems on BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Audi, Volkswagen, and Porsche. Each brand has its own HVAC diagnostic software and system quirks — we have the tools and experience for all of them.
