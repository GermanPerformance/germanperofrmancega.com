VW TIMING CHAIN
COMPLETE KIT SERVICE

We never replace just the tensioner. A complete timing chain service includes every component that wears together — because a cheap fix today leads to a catastrophic failure tomorrow.

01
Timing Chain Replacement
We replace the timing chain with an OEM or OEM-equivalent unit from INA or Iwis — the same suppliers VW uses from the factory. A stretched chain causes incorrect cam timing, reduced power, and eventually catastrophic engine damage.
02
Tensioner & Guide Replacement
The plastic timing chain tensioner on the EA888 is the primary failure point. It loses oil pressure when cold and allows the chain to slap. We replace the tensioner, upper guide, and lower guide as a complete kit — never just one piece.
03
Cam Phaser Service
Worn or stuck cam phasers cause rough idle, poor throttle response, and timing-related fault codes. We inspect and replace cam phasers as part of a complete timing chain service on high-mileage EA888 engines.
04
Cam & Crank Seals
With the timing cover removed, we inspect cam and crank seals for seepage. An oil leak onto a fresh timing chain will degrade it prematurely — we replace seals proactively while we already have access.
05
VCDS Diagnostic Scan
Before and after the repair, we perform a full VCDS scan to confirm all timing-related fault codes are cleared and no new codes are present. We also verify cam and crank timing values are within spec after installation.
06
Oil Change Included
Timing chain work generates metal particles. We perform a full oil and filter change after every timing chain job — using VW-spec oil — to flush any debris before it circulates through your freshly rebuilt engine's lubrication system.

Common Questions

VW TIMING CHAIN FAQ

My VW rattles for a few seconds on cold start — is that the timing chain?

Almost certainly yes. On the EA888 2.0T engine, the plastic timing chain tensioner loses oil pressure when the engine sits overnight. On cold start it takes a moment to build pressure, during which the chain slaps against the guides and creates a rattling noise. It usually disappears within a few seconds — but this is a serious warning sign. A failed tensioner leads to a jumped or broken timing chain and a destroyed engine. Don't ignore it.

Which VW models have the EA888 timing chain problem?

The EA888 2.0T engine is found in the Golf GTI, Golf R, Jetta GLI, Passat, Tiguan, and CC from approximately 2008–2014 (Gen 1 and Gen 2 EA888). The later Gen 3 EA888 (2015+) has an improved tensioner design but can still develop issues at high mileage. If you're unsure which engine you have, call us with your VIN and we'll confirm.

Can I just replace the tensioner or do I need the full kit?

We always recommend the complete kit — tensioner, chain, upper guide, lower guide, and cam phasers on high-mileage engines. A tensioner-only repair on a stretched chain is a temporary fix that will require the full job again soon. The chain, tensioner, and guides all wear together. Doing the complete job once is more cost-effective than two partial repairs.

What happens if the VW timing chain breaks?

The EA888 is an interference engine — meaning if the timing chain breaks or jumps, the pistons and valves collide at high speed. The result is bent valves, a damaged cylinder head, and often a totaled engine. Repair costs typically run $4,000–$8,000 or more. A timing chain service is a fraction of that cost and prevents the failure entirely.

How long does a VW timing chain repair take?

A complete EA888 timing chain kit replacement typically takes 6–8 hours. We recommend dropping off in the morning and expecting an overnight or next-day completion depending on parts availability. Call us to confirm timing for your specific model.

Will an oil change help with the timing chain rattle?

Fresh oil can slightly reduce the rattle by improving oil pressure delivery to the tensioner — but it does not fix the underlying problem. If the tensioner is failing or the chain is stretched, no amount of oil changes will prevent eventual failure. The rattle is a warning sign that requires mechanical repair, not a fluid service.
